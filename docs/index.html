It all runs off a few keys

F8 and F9 are both on all the time, so you just use whichever one fits what you're doing. Don't like those keys? Change them to anything you want.

  • F8

    Hold to talk

    Hold it while you talk. When you let go, TuxTalk types what you said wherever your cursor is.

  • F9

    Tap to start, tap to stop

    For the longer stuff. Double-tap it and it goes hands-free, then stops by itself when you go quiet. Say "press enter" at the end and your message gets sent too.

  • Ctrl+Alt+P

    Type it again

    Something grabbed the focus and your words went into the wrong window? Click where they belong and press this.

  • Shift+F8

    Voice editing Labs

    Select some text, hold it and say what to change, like "make this more formal". It's off until you turn it on.

  • Right CtrlorRight AltorScroll Lock

    Keys that don't need Fn

    On a lot of laptops the F-keys only work while you hold Fn, which makes holding F8 a two-handed job. Pick one of these in Preferences, General and you don't need Fn. Right Ctrl and Right Alt stop working as Ctrl and Alt once they're TuxTalk's, and if Right Alt is your AltGr key the list tells you which characters you'd lose, like @ and €. TuxTalk may ask to update its hotkey helper first. That's your password once and no restart.

The AI that catches "no wait" and "scratch that" is a small model that runs on your computer. If it changes anything besides the correction, your words get typed exactly the way you said them. There are voice commands for fixing things too, like "undo last word" or "delete last three sentences".

It picks a setup for your computer

The first time you start it, TuxTalk looks at your graphics card and the language you speak and picks a setup for you. One button shows every other choice, with how big each download is.

The default speech model is NVIDIA's Parakeet. It's fast even on an ordinary processor and handles English plus 24 European languages. For anything else there's OpenAI's Whisper, which knows 99 languages, from Tiny all the way up to Large-v3.

Got a graphics card? NVIDIA, AMD or Intel, TuxTalk can use it. That's a 22 MB engine plus a 669 MB copy of Parakeet made for the graphics card. It times your card against your processor first and only switches if the card is really faster. On an NVIDIA graphics card there's also a bigger 1.4 GB option, but that one only runs the Whisper models.

Teach it the words you use

Names and part numbers, the stuff a speech model has never heard of. Put them in Your Words, in the Words tab, and they come out spelled your way. It steers Parakeet and Whisper both, on the processor or the graphics card. Parakeet kept writing my app's name as "Tuck's talk". Now it's TuxTalk.

If a word still comes out wrong, say "fix that word" right after. A window opens on what you just said, you click the word and type it the right way, and from then on it's fixed. It fixes it in your text too, and the Words tab keeps each fix under the word it belongs to.

Snippets are your own shortcuts. Say "my email" and get your whole address typed out.

Transcribe a File

Got a recording or a video? Hand it to TuxTalk from the tray menu and you get the text back. MP3, M4A, WAV, MP4, MKV and a bunch more all work.

It uses the same model you dictate with, so there's nothing new to download, and you can keep dictating while it works. On my NVIDIA card with Parakeet, three and a half minutes of talking took about nine seconds.

You can copy the text or save it as subtitles for the video. The subtitle timing can be a second or two off, which is fine for most things but I wouldn't use it for a film. It also keeps your last 10, so closing the window doesn't lose anything.

TuxTalk Labs

Labs is where new stuff goes while I'm still working on it. It works, it's just not finished, so it stays off until you turn it on. You'll find it at the bottom of Preferences, Advanced, with a link right there to tell me how it's going.

Type at pause

Each phrase gets typed when you pause, instead of all at once when you're done. Put it together with hands-free on F9 and you can say a message, look it over, say "edit that, make it friendlier", and say "press enter" to send it. No keyboard at all. guelz built this one.

Voice editing

Select some text, hold Shift+F8 and say what you want changed. Or skip the selecting: right after you dictate, say "edit that, make it shorter". There's no fixed wording, and Ctrl+Z puts it back if you don't like it. Jobs like all caps or taking out commas are done exactly in code. Rewording goes to a small AI model that runs on your computer, and that one's a download you only get if you turn it on.

Select and move by voice

Say "select the last sentence" or "go to the end" on its own and TuxTalk does it instead of typing it. Put that together with voice editing and you can select your last sentence and say "make it more formal" without reaching for the mouse.

An indicator that follows your window

The recording indicator can sit near the bottom of whatever window you're talking into, instead of one spot on the screen.

And a bunch of smaller stuff

Pauses your music

Music and videos pause while you dictate and pick back up when you're done. I had a YouTube video going once and it turned "isn't it" into "Venezuela".

Turns other sounds down

For what pausing can't reach, like a Discord call or a game. It's off until you turn it on, and it only changes what you hear. A call still hears your mic.

Says when the mic is silent

Muted mic, unplugged headset, wrong input picked? TuxTalk tells you it heard nothing but silence instead of just typing nothing.

Sends the message for you

Turn on Send each dictation automatically in Preferences, Advanced and TuxTalk presses Enter after a short countdown, so a chat message just goes. Tap F8 during the countdown if you want to fix something first. It won't send in a text editor, where Enter only starts a new line, and if you switch windows before it's done counting nothing gets sent.

Takes out the ums

There's a setting in Preferences, Advanced that drops "um" and "uh" and the words you say twice by accident, so "um, so the the van is ready" comes out "So the van is ready." It leaves the ones you mean alone, like "no, no, no".

Easy on a laptop battery

On a laptop it lets go of the speech model when you haven't dictated for a while. F8 still works, the first dictation after just takes a few seconds longer. On a desktop that's off.

Recent Dictations

Your last 20 are in the tray menu, one click to copy. They're only kept in memory and they're gone when you log out.

Your Stats

How many words you've dictated and about how much typing that saved you. Only the numbers get kept, never what you said.

Settings backup

Save all your settings and words to one file and load it on another computer. It keeps your last 10 settings on its own too, in case a change goes wrong.

An indicator you can style

Orb, waveform, frequency bars or radial, any color, anywhere on the screen. It moves with your voice so you know it's hearing you.

Only the languages you speak

Whisper picks from 99 languages and on a short dictation it sometimes guesses wrong. Tick the ones you actually speak and it sticks to those.

Stays out of password boxes

If your cursor is in a password field, TuxTalk types nothing and says why. That works in most GTK apps, in KDE apps and Firefox with accessibility on, and in your desktop's own admin password prompt.

It tells you why

If a dictation can't be typed into a window, TuxTalk says why and what fixes it instead of just doing nothing.

Report a Problem

Help, Problems has a button that puts together what I'd ask you for anyway, your system and settings and the end of TuxTalk's log. Your username's taken out and none of what you dictated is in it. You see all of it first, then you can copy it or save it, or open a GitHub issue with it already filled in. Nothing gets sent unless you do that yourself.

No tray? Still works

On a desktop with no system tray, like plain GNOME, opening TuxTalk from your apps brings up its menu in a small window.

A command for your own shortcut

tuxtalk --toggle, plus --start and --stop, if you'd rather use your desktop's shortcuts or a script.

More about who's behind it and how it's made

Nothing you say ever leaves your computer.

TuxTalk turns your speech into text right there on your computer. There's no account and no cloud, and once the speech model is downloaded it works with the internet unplugged.

The privacy page has the details, including the few times TuxTalk does go online and why.

  • Only TuxTalk's little hotkey helper reads your keyboard, and it only passes on TuxTalk's own hotkeys. Other programs don't get to see what you type.

  • What you dictate doesn't go in the log file. There's a setting to log it for troubleshooting, and it's off.

  • The AI corrections are optional and off until you turn them on. If you do, the AI runs on your computer too.

  • Your Stats keeps numbers only. Recent Dictations are held in memory and gone when you log out.

AUR page

The Download buttons take you to the newest release on GitHub. Grab the file that ends in .AppImage, .deb or .rpm. What changed in each version is in the changelog.

The first time it runs, TuxTalk asks for your admin password once. That sets up its hotkey helper, the one small piece that reads the keyboard, and lets TuxTalk type into other apps. No restart needed. On a system without systemd it uses the input group instead, tells you why, and then it does need a restart.

The AppImage needs fusermount, which almost every desktop already has. If it won't start, install the fuse3 package. No Flatpak yet. If you'd like one, vote in the poll.

Works on your desktop

TuxTalk's hotkey helper reads the key straight from the keyboard instead of asking the desktop for it, so hold-to-talk works on Wayland and X11 with no setup. On GNOME you get a proper panel menu, everywhere else a tray icon, and on a desktop with neither, a small menu window.

Uninstalling

Open Preferences, go to Updates & Backup and click Uninstall TuxTalk… at the bottom. It takes out TuxTalk and everything it set up on your computer: the program, the hotkey helper, the speech models and everything else it downloaded (CUDA libraries included), starting at login, its menu entry and the GNOME extension. It asks for your password once. Your settings and word list stay unless you tick the box to delete them too.

If TuxTalk won't open, the same thing runs from a terminal. For the AppImage it's ~/AppImages/TuxTalk.AppImage --uninstall, and for the .deb, .rpm and AUR:

tuxtalk --uninstall

Removing just the package with apt, dnf or pacman takes the hotkey helper with it, but leaves your settings and the downloads in your home folder. If you'd rather do it all by hand, or you have a version older than 0.17, the step-by-step commands are in the README.
