Dictation apps for Linux, compared

Every free dictation app for Linux I know of, side by side, as fairly as I can put it.

TuxTalk isn't the only one

A few years ago there was almost nothing for dictation on Linux. Now there are several good free apps, and most of them run offline. Here's how they line up, as fairly as I can put it.

Weighing one in particular? There are side-by-side pages for TuxTalk or Myna, Dragon, and Wispr Flow and Superwhisper.

Which one?

Myna is Canonical's own, a technology preview and an optional feature in Ubuntu 26.10, and it shows the words as you talk. If you're on Ubuntu with GNOME and want the one Ubuntu supports, give it a try. There's a page on Myna and TuxTalk with more.

Handy is a nice pick if you also use Windows or a Mac, since it runs on all three.

Speech Note also reads text aloud and translates, so it's more than dictation.

Voxtype fits right in on Hyprland, Sway and River.

nerd-dictation is a small script for people who like to wire things up themselves.

TuxTalk is for hold F8 and talk on whatever desktop you're on, with nothing to set up in your desktop's settings, and it types straight into the app. It has Your Words and Fix a Word for names it gets wrong, an optional local AI for "no wait" corrections, Transcribe a File, and voice editing and Type at pause in TuxTalk Labs. Only TuxTalk's small hotkey helper reads your keyboard. It's Linux only.

Prices and details are as of October 2026, from each product's own pages where I could find them. They change, so check there before you decide. If something here is out of date, tell me in Discussions and I'll fix it.
